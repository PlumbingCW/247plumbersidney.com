The problem

Water in the street, water in the basement

On 17 May 2019 the National Weather Service reported flash flooding in Jackson Center. Water covered Main Street and Pike Street, and 2 to 3 feet stood on Robb Avenue. A month later, on 18 June, high water closed SR 274.

A storm like that can push water into low basements and floor drains within minutes. A sump pump is the first line of defense inside the house, and a sewer lateral that backs up when public lines are full is the second problem. Both need a plumber, not a mop.

The village keeps its own flood damage reduction ordinances. Ask the village office at 122 E. Pike Street, 937-596-6314, before you do any work below the flood line.

While you wait

Do this before the plumber arrives

Water from a pipe

Turn the main shutoff clockwise and open one faucet at the lowest level to drain what remains.

Water from outside

Do not walk into a flooded basement if the panel, furnace or outlets are in the water. Call 911 if anyone is at risk.

Sewage on the floor

Avoid contact, keep children and pets away, and do not run more water down the drains.

Housing

How old the houses are, and why it matters

A census survey puts the median year built in Jackson Center at 1979, and about half of the 593 housing units were built before 1980. Roughly a quarter predate 1960. It is an estimate for a small village.

Houses from that period often have their first water heater replaced and shutoff valves that have not turned in decades. Test yours twice a year, because a valve that sticks during a flood becomes your biggest problem.

Village water

Four wells, no lead lines, and the tap

The village water comes from four wells in a well field at the south end of the village, between 68 and 186 feet deep. Total chlorine was about 1.3 ppm and fluoride 0.99 ppm. The village says it has no lead service lines.

Hardness is not stated, so test your tap if scale builds on fixtures. Tiger Trail Park sits on the well field grounds behind Grace Evangelical Lutheran Church off SR 65.

FAQs

Common questions

Do you cover every ZIP code in Jackson Center?

Jackson Center uses ZIP 45334, which also stretches into Auglaize and Logan counties. The nearby village of Kettlersville has its own PO box ZIP, 45336. We take calls for both from our Sidney base.

Is emergency service available in Jackson Center?

Yes. We answer around the clock, including nights, weekends and holidays. A burst pipe or flooded basement is dispatched from Sidney by SR 274 and SR 65, and we tell you what to do while the plumber travels.

Does Jackson Center have lead service lines?

The village says it has no lead service lines. Your own plumbing inside the house is separate, so ask if you are unsure, or test your tap if you want to confirm what is in your water.

How do I know if the sump pump has failed?

Look for a silent pump and water in the pit, or a pump that runs and never shuts off. Check the plug and the float, and if neither fixes it, call. Do not stand in standing water to test it.

Who issues permits for plumbing work in the village?

The Shelby County Building Department covers Jackson Center for building work. Plumbing permits come from the Sidney-Shelby County Health Department at 937-498-7249, so ask which office your job needs.

What if the road is closed by high water?

Tell the dispatcher your address and a second route in. High water has closed SR 274 before, and an alternate approach may be needed. Do not drive through standing water to meet us.
