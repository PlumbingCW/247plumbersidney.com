Housing era

Houses built before 1960 and what runs inside them

The Piqua-Caldwell Historic District was listed on the National Register in 1985 and takes in parts of North Main, Wayne, Downing, Caldwell, West Ash, Greene, North, Mound and Park Avenue. Homes in and around that area are among the oldest in the city, and old homes often keep their original drain lines under the floor.

Cast iron drains corrode from the inside, and the rough surface catches grease and paper long before the pipe cracks. If you live in an older house, a drain that gurgles or backs up twice in a season deserves a camera look, not another bottle of chemical cleaner.

Water source

Where Piqua's tap water comes from

Piqua Municipal Water draws on three surface-water sources. In the 2025 report, about 58 percent of supply came from the Great Miami River, about 30 percent from the Gravel Pit and about 12 percent from the Hydraulic System. The city adds chlorine and fluoride.

The report does not print a hardness figure, so test your tap or ask the city utilities department if scale on fixtures is your question. Ask the city what material your own service line is made of.

Flood history

The river, the levee and wet basements

In 1913 the Great Miami River flooded Piqua. Local records say 44 people died, most of them in the Shawnee district, and about 2,000 of nearly 13,500 homes took water. The Miami Conservancy District now maintains about 2.8 miles of levee in the city.

A levee protects the town from the river, not your basement from groundwater or a backed-up lateral. After heavy rain, a sump pump that has sat idle for months is the thing most likely to fail, and a sewer backup needs its own response. Our sump pump repair visits include checking the check valve and the discharge line.

Services

Calls we take from Piqua, in the order they come up

Sewer and lateral problems

Backups in older homes usually trace to the line under the house or yard. Our sewer line repair crews scope the pipe, explain what they see, and fix the failed section. Emergency dispatch is available around the clock.

Hidden leaks and burst pipes

A water bill that jumps or a warm patch on the floor points to a supply leak. We shut the water down and use leak detection to find the spot without tearing out half a wall.

Clogs that keep returning

A drain that clears and then slows again needs more than a snake. Drain cleaning with a camera shows whether grease, roots or a broken pipe is behind it.

Water heaters in older basements

Rust at the base of a tank means it is near the end. We handle water heater replacement the same day when parts and tanks are on hand.

Burst pipes and frozen lines

When a pipe splits, burst pipe repair begins with stopping the flow and ends with a tested repair.

Who issues plumbing permits in Piqua?

Piqua is in Miami County, so ask Miami County Public Health, whose plumbing office is at 937-573-3534. Rules differ from the Shelby County process, so ask before the work starts instead of afterward.

Can an old cast iron drain be repaired without digging up the floor?

Sometimes. A camera shows where the pipe has failed, and a section can often be repaired or lined from the cleanout. Whether that works depends on what the camera finds, and we explain the options before any cutting begins.

My basement took on water after heavy rain. Is that a plumber's job?

Often it is. Check whether the sump pump is running and whether the discharge line is clear. If sewage came up through a floor drain, avoid contact and call us, because that is a sewer problem, not a rain problem.

Does your crew work in Troy as well?

Yes. Troy sits close to Piqua, and calls from the Troy ZIP 45373 are dispatched from the same Sidney line. Mention your cross streets so the dispatcher can route the nearest available plumber.
