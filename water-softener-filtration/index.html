Water softener and filtration work is the choice, installation and repair of equipment that changes what comes out of your tap. A softener trades hardness minerals for sodium or potassium, a filter catches particles or tastes, and reverse osmosis pushes water through a membrane at one faucet. Our line at (855) 575-2890 is open 24/7.

The right equipment depends on what is in your water, so the first step is to test your tap. Buying equipment before you know the problem is the most common mistake we see.

This page explains what each type does, what it cannot do, and how to decide before you spend money.

Start here

Test your tap before you buy anything

A water test tells you what to treat. Hardness, chlorine taste, iron and sediment all call for different equipment, and one device does not fix them all. Ask your water utility for its latest report, and use a certified lab for a test of your own tap if you want numbers from your house.

If you are on the City of Sidney system, the city's 2022 water quality report listed total hardness of about 113 ppm, in the moderately hard range on the USGS scale. The City's 2025 report lists chlorine as the disinfectant. These are reports from different years, so treat the hardness number as history and test your tap for today.

Wells

Private well? Call the health department first

If your water comes from a private well, the Sidney-Shelby County Health Department permits and inspects private water systems. Call 937-498-7249 before you choose equipment, and have the water tested first. Bacteria, nitrate and other well problems are not solved by a softener, and a wrong choice can hide a real hazard.

The choices

Softener, filter or reverse osmosis: what each one does

These three are not interchangeable. Each does one job well and leaves other jobs undone.

Water softener

It swaps calcium and magnesium for sodium or potassium, which reduces scale on fixtures, glass and heaters. It does not remove chlorine, bacteria or most chemicals, and it is not made to make water cleaner to drink.

Whole-house sediment or carbon filter

A sediment filter catches grit and rust. A carbon filter reduces chlorine taste and odor. Neither one removes hardness, and both need cartridges changed on schedule.

Reverse osmosis at the sink

A membrane and storage tank clean water for drinking and cooking at one faucet. It removes many dissolved solids but wastes some water and does not treat the rest of the house.

Pairing them

Many homes use a softener for the whole house, a carbon filter for taste and an RO unit at the kitchen faucet. Each device does a separate job.

Repair

Repairing a water conditioner that stopped working

A softener that no longer softens usually has a resin bed that is spent, a brine tank where the salt has bridged into a crust, or a valve head that no longer cycles. Hard water at the tap despite a full brine tank points to a bridge or a stuck valve. Many units also have a bypass valve that was left open after service.

We check these simple causes first, and we tell you when replacement is the better value. A wet floor around the unit may be a loose drain line or a leaking fitting, and leak detection helps when the source is not obvious.

FAQs

Common questions

Does a water softener make water safe to drink?

No. A softener removes hardness minerals, not bacteria or most contaminants. If drinking quality is your concern, ask about a filter or reverse osmosis, and test your tap first.

How hard is the water in Sidney?

The city's 2022 water quality report listed total hardness of about 113 ppm, in the moderately hard range on the USGS scale. That is a past report, so test your tap for your own current number.

I have a private well. Where do I start?

Call the Sidney-Shelby County Health Department at 937-498-7249, which permits and inspects private water systems. Have the water tested before you choose any equipment.

Why is my softener still giving me hard water?

The brine tank may have a salt bridge, the resin may be spent, the bypass may be open or the valve head may not be cycling. We check those in order, starting with the simple causes.

Does a softener remove chlorine?

No. The City's 2025 report lists chlorine as the disinfectant. A carbon filter reduces chlorine taste and smell, and a softener does not, so pick the device that matches the complaint you actually have.

Do you work on water softeners at night or on weekends?

Our phone line is open 24/7. Equipment calls are usually booked for the next open appointment, but a leak from a softener line or valve is dispatched right away, including nights and weekends.
