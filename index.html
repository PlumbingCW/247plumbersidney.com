Safety first

Smell gas? Leave the building first

A gas smell comes before any plumbing call. Get everyone outside, do not touch light switches or start anything electrical, and call 911 and the gas utility whose emergency number is printed on your bill. Call us afterward for water heater or pipe work, once the utility says the building is safe.

Local plumbing

Sidney homes and the plumbing they call about

Sidney sits on the Great Miami River in Shelby County, and the river's history matters to basements. The National Weather Service gauge at Sidney recorded a crest of 19.6 feet in March 1913 and 15.61 feet in December 2013. If your basement sits low or close to the river, a working sump pump with a backup is worth setting up before the next wet spring.

The City runs an inflow and infiltration program for sewer laterals. Where an owner receives a notice, the building drain and sewer must be inspected, and the owner is responsible for the pipe from the building to the City's main and tap. A camera inspection answers that question and shows what any repair should cover.

City water comes from wellfields and from intakes on Tawawa Creek and the Great Miami River. The City's 2022 water quality report listed total hardness of about 113 ppm, in the moderately hard range on the USGS scale. Over the years that leaves scale inside tanks, so a water heater that has started to rumble is worth a look before it fails.

Downtown, the Courthouse Square Historic District and the Louis Sullivan designed Peoples Federal building at 101 E Court St show how old parts of town are. Older buildings often mean older piping and tight access, so we ask about the age of the house on the first call. The same line serves Port Jefferson, Lockington and the rest of Shelby County.

Before you hire

How to check any plumber in Shelby County

Anyone doing plumbing work in Shelby County needs a plumbing permit from the Sidney-Shelby County Health Department, and plumbers register with the health district each year. Call 937-498-7249 to ask whether a contractor is on the registered list and whether your job counts as minor repair.

Commercial plumbing work needs a state licence from the Ohio Construction Industry Licensing Board, which publishes an online license lookup. Checking takes a few minutes and applies to us and to every other company you call.

FAQs

Emergency plumber questions

Is there an emergency plumber in Sidney, OH open at night?

Yes. The line at (855) 575-2890 is open 24/7, including nights, weekends and holidays. Call, describe what is happening, and we dispatch to Sidney and nearby Shelby County towns as soon as a plumber is free.

What should I do while I wait for a plumber?

Close the main shutoff if water is running, stop using drains if sewage is backing up, and keep people away from wet electrical areas. Move valuables out of the wet area and take photos for your insurer.

How do I know the price before work starts?

After the plumber sees the problem, you get a clear price and the options before any work begins. If the job grows, you hear about it first. We do not promise a free estimate, and we do not quote exact figures over the phone.

Which towns do you serve besides Sidney?

Piqua, Anna, Botkins, Fort Loramie and Jackson Center have their own pages, and the same 24/7 line covers the rest of Shelby County. If you are farther out, call and ask.

What if I smell gas?

Leave the building at once, then call 911 and the gas utility number on your bill from outside. Do not call us first and do not use switches or phones inside. Once the utility clears the building, we can help with the plumbing side.

Does homeowner insurance cover a burst pipe?

Policies often treat a sudden burst differently from slow, long-term seepage, so ask your insurer what applies to yours. Photos taken before cleanup and the repair invoice both help your claim.

Do you handle commercial plumbing emergencies?

Yes. Shops, restaurants and offices in Sidney can call the same 24/7 line, and the commercial plumbing page explains what we need to know about the building before we arrive.
