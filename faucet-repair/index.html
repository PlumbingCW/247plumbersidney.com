Act first

When a faucet becomes an emergency

A steady drip is a nuisance and can wait for a daytime appointment. Water that sprays, a handle that spins without stopping the flow, or a supply line that bursts under the sink needs action now. Each of these can put a lot of water on a cabinet floor in minutes.

Shut off the supply stop valves under the sink by turning them clockwise. If the stops will not turn, or water keeps coming from the valve stem, go to the main shutoff. In Sidney the property owner is responsible for the service line from the meter pit or curb stop into the building, so it helps to know where your own main valve sits before you ever need it.

Once the water is stopped, move anything valuable out of the cabinet, open the doors so the space can dry, and leave the faucet alone. Cranking a failed handle harder usually breaks the stem, which turns a cartridge job into a valve replacement.

Common faults

Faucet problems we repair most

Handle drips or leaks at the base

Worn O-rings and seats let water creep up the body of the faucet. Replacing the O-rings and seat is usually quick, and it keeps the cabinet below dry.

Water will not turn off

A failed cartridge or washer cannot seal. The cartridge is the working part of most modern faucets, and swapping it restores a full shutoff without replacing the fixture.

Low flow from one spout

Mineral buildup clogs the aerator and the cartridge ports. The city's 2022 water quality report listed total hardness of about 113 ppm, which is in the moderately hard range, so scale shows up on fixtures over the years.

Shower or tub valve leaks behind the wall

A leaking shower valve can wet the wall behind it. We shut the water off, open the access panel if there is one, and replace the cartridge or valve body as the repair requires.

Supply line or stop valve drips

Braided supply lines and old multi-turn stops fail with age. They are cheap parts that cause expensive damage when they let go, so replacing them during a repair is often sensible.

Hidden failures

Valves, supply lines and fixtures that cause the worst leaks

Many callers describe a broken or burst supply valve under a sink or toilet. These small brass or plastic parts hold full house pressure all day, and an old one can fail without warning. If you see rust-colored crust, green corrosion or a damp cabinet floor, the valve is already on its way out.

Replacing a stop valve means shutting off water to that fixture or to the whole house, cutting the old valve off the pipe, and installing a quarter-turn valve with a new line. A quarter-turn stop is easier to close in an emergency than the multi-turn valves found on older homes.

Tub and shower trim leaks deserve their own mention. When a showerhead drips or a tub spout will not stop, the cause is usually the valve cartridge or the diverter, and it can be fixed from the front of the wall in many cases. If the valve body itself leaks inside the wall, the repair is bigger, and we explain the options before we cut anything.

Local note

Faucets and water quality in Sidney

The City of Sidney supplies water from groundwater wellfields and from surface intakes on Tawawa Creek and the Great Miami River, and the 2025 consumer confidence report lists chlorine as the disinfectant. Minerals, not chlorine, are what most often leave scale on a faucet aerator.

If your spout flow has dropped, unscrew the aerator and look for white or tan grit. Soaking it in vinegar often clears it, and if the cartridge ports are scaled up we can clean or replace the cartridge. Ask your water utility for the latest water quality report if you want the current numbers for your address.

FAQs

Common questions

Do you handle faucet repair emergencies at night or on weekends in Sidney?

Yes. Our line is open 24/7 and we dispatch for faucets, valves and supply lines that cannot be shut off, including nights, weekends and holidays. If the leak is slow, we book the next open appointment and tell you how to hold it until we arrive.

Can I fix a dripping faucet myself?

Often, yes. Shut off the supply, take photos as you disassemble, and bring the old cartridge to a supplier to match it. Call a plumber if the shutoff valve will not close, if parts are corroded to the body, or if the leak is inside the wall.

Should I repair or replace an old faucet?

If the body is sound and parts are available, repair is usually the better value. If the finish is worn through, the body leaks, or parts are obsolete, replacement makes more sense. We lay out both options before work starts.

Why does my faucet drip even after a new washer?

The valve seat may be pitted, which keeps a new washer from sealing against it. Dressing the seat or replacing it usually solves the drip, and we check the seat first on compression-style faucets so you are not paying for the same repair twice.

What if the shutoff under my sink will not turn?

Do not force it. Close the main water shutoff where the line enters the house and call us. We can replace a seized stop valve and the supply line during the same visit.

Do you replace shower and tub valves?

Yes. Many shower leaks are fixed by replacing the cartridge from the front of the valve. When the valve body is bad, we explain what access is needed before we cut into the wall.
