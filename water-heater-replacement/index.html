Water heater replacement means removing a failed tank and installing a new one that fits your household, your fuel and your piping. The visit usually starts with a quick look to confirm that a repair would not be enough.

Failed tank

A tank that is leaking through the shell

A steel tank rusts from the inside out. Over the years the protective anode rod is consumed, and once it is gone the tank wall itself starts to corrode. When water seeps from the jacket or the bottom seam, the damage is already done, and tightening fittings will not help.

Turn off the cold supply, kill the power or gas, and open a hot tap somewhere upstairs to let the tank drain a little. Then move belongings out of the puddle. If the floor drain is blocked or the heater is in a finished space, mop up early so the subfloor does not stay wet.

If the water is only dripping from a pipe fitting or the relief valve, the tank may be fine. Our water heater repair page explains those cases, and we check for them before recommending a new unit.

Age and condition

Repair it or replace it

Tank life varies, but heaters often last roughly 8 to 12 years. Use age as a guide, not a rule, because water, usage and maintenance all change the number.

Repair usually makes sense when

  • The tank is dry and the leak is at a fitting, drain valve or relief valve.
  • The unit is on the younger side of that 8 to 12 year range.
  • The problem is an element, thermostat, igniter or thermocouple.
  • There is no rust at the base and no moisture under the insulation.

Replacement usually makes sense when

  • Water is coming through the tank body or the bottom seam.
  • The unit is at or past the typical life range and has needed several repairs.
  • Rusty hot water, a rumbling tank and a corroded base all show up together.
  • The heater is too small for the household and runs out in the morning.
Sizing and fuel

Choosing a replacement that fits your house

Size by household

A tank sized for two adults will struggle with a family of five. Bigger households, extra bathrooms and a soaking tub usually call for a larger tank or a higher recovery rate. We size it with you on the visit.

Gas or electric

Swapping fuel is a bigger job than swapping like for like. A gas unit needs a vent path and a gas line. An electric unit needs a circuit with the right capacity. Keeping the same fuel is usually faster.

Standard tank or hybrid

A hybrid heat pump unit uses less energy but needs clearance, a condensate drain and enough air space. A standard tank is simpler and fits more utility closets.

Tank or tankless

A tankless unit gives continuous hot water but changes venting, gas line size and water treatment needs. Our tankless water heater page covers that in detail.

Before we arrive

Getting ready for the plumber

A few minutes of preparation saves time on the install.

1

Clear a path

Move storage away from the heater and the route to it. Plumbers need room to carry a new tank in and the old one out.

2

Find the shutoffs

Locate the cold supply valve above the tank and the main shutoff for the house. Note whether the heater uses a gas shutoff or a breaker.

3

Take photos

Photograph the tank label, the flue pipe, the piping and any damage. The label tells us the capacity and fuel, and the photos help your insurer.

4

Know who will be home

An adult should be on site to approve the work. Tell us about pets, locked doors and any stairs between the garage and the basement.

Permit question

Permits for replacement jobs in Sidney

Plumbing work in Shelby County is permitted through the Sidney-Shelby County Health Department, and the rules for a minor repair differ from the rules for a replacement. The health department's regulation does not list a water heater swap by name, so we do not guess.

Call the Sidney-Shelby County Health Department at 937-498-7249 to ask whether your job needs a permit. That one call can save you a failed inspection. Plumbers working in the county register with the health district each year, so you can ask for its current list.

Urgent

After-hours failures: what to do tonight

If a tank fails at night, do not try to run the old heater to stretch it a few more days. A heater that has leaked through the shell can fail suddenly, and scalding water or an electrical short are real risks. Shut it down, and use a kettle or the stove for basics.

Call the 24/7 line and we will tell you what is possible the same day or first thing in the morning. A planned install can often be scheduled for the next open appointment, and we can discuss whether a temporary fix makes sense while you decide.

FAQs

Common questions

Do you handle water heater replacement emergencies at night or on weekends in Sidney?

Yes. Our line is open 24/7, including nights, weekends and holidays. When a tank has failed, we tell you on the call whether a same-day replacement is possible, and what to do to limit damage until then.

How do I know if my water heater needs replacing?

Water leaking through the shell or bottom seam almost always means replacement. Age, rust in the hot water, a rumbling tank and repeated repairs are other signs. Tanks often last roughly 8 to 12 years, but that is only a guide.

What size water heater do I need?

It depends on the number of people, the number of bathrooms and your peak hot water use. We look at how your household uses water and suggest a size on site rather than guess over the phone.

Do I need a permit to replace a water heater in Sidney?

Call the Sidney-Shelby County Health Department at 937-498-7249 to ask whether your job needs a permit. Rules differ from minor repair, and the health department can tell you what applies to your situation.

Should I switch from gas to electric or keep the same fuel?

Keeping the same fuel is usually faster and simpler. Changing fuel means new venting, gas piping or electrical work, so it is best treated as a planned project. We can walk through the trade-offs on a visit.

What should I do with the old tank after the swap is done?

Ask about removal when you book, so the plan for the old tank is settled before the visit. Until then, keep the area clear and do not try to move a full tank yourself, since it is heavy and can spill.
