Sewer Inspection in Sidney, OH

A camera runs through your sewer line so you can see the actual condition of the pipe before you buy, repair or replace anything.

A sewer inspection is a camera survey of the pipe that carries wastewater from your house out to the City's main. A small waterproof camera on a flexible rod travels the line, and you see the inside of the pipe on a monitor as it goes.

Most people book one for a reason: they are buying a house, a drain keeps backing up, or the City sent a notice asking for a video. In each case the camera replaces guessing about a pipe you cannot see.

Inspections are planned visits, and we schedule them at your convenience, with same-day appointments when available. Our 24/7 line is still open if the line turns into a backup before your visit.

The method

How we run the camera through your line

The visit follows a simple order, and most of it is done from one access point.

1

Find the way in

We use a cleanout if there is one. Without one, we may pull a toilet or work through a roof vent.

2

Clear what blocks the view

If grease or debris hides the wall, the line may need a cable pass or cleaning before the camera can see.

3

Feed the camera

The camera moves toward the City's main while we watch the monitor and note anything unusual.

4

Locate and note problems

When the image shows damage, we note how far along the line it sits, so a repair crew knows where to dig.

Reading the video

What the picture shows

Offset joints

Two pipe ends that no longer line up. Waste catches on the ledge, and roots find the gap.

Roots

Fine hair-like roots at a joint, or a thick mass that fills the pipe. They show where the pipe has been breached.

Bellies

A low spot where the pipe sags and holds water. Waste settles in it and clogs form there again and again.

Cracks and breaks

Hairline cracks, longitudinal splits or missing pieces. They can let ground water in and sewage out.

Scale and grease

Rust scale in iron pipe and layers of grease reduce the open diameter and explain slow drains.

Before you buy

Buying a home with an older sewer line

A home inspection normally checks what is visible. The sewer lateral runs underground, and a failing one can be an expensive surprise after closing. A camera look before you buy gives you evidence to ask for a repair, a price change, or to walk away.

Older houses are the main concern. The City's own plan estimates that roughly 30 percent or more of its housing was built before 1950, and a house of that age may have clay or cast iron pipe. If the seller will allow it, schedule the camera before the inspection period ends.

Why it keeps happening

After repeated backups

If a drain has been cabled several times and keeps returning, the cause is almost never bad luck. A camera finds the sag, the root entry or the broken joint that a cable cannot show. That evidence lets you decide between sewer line cleaning on a schedule and a real repair.

It also keeps you from paying for hydro jetting on a pipe that cannot take the pressure. A sound pipe can be jetted. A cracked one needs a different plan, and our sewer line repair page lays out the options.

Local note

The City of Sidney's inflow and infiltration program

The City runs an inflow and infiltration program under its ordinances, to keep clean water out of the sanitary sewer. In areas that receive a notice, owners must have the building drain and building sewer professionally inspected by video and send the report to the City. If you got a notice, follow it. Other project areas have their own notices, and we do not know your deadline.

Call the Underground Utilities office at 937-498-8157 with questions about your notice. We run the camera and give you the video and notes from the visit. Ask us what form you will receive before you book, and check with the City what it needs, since we do not assume its format.

FAQs

Common questions

How long does a sewer camera inspection take?

It depends on line length, access and how much debris is in the pipe. A straightforward run is usually done in a single visit, and we tell you what to expect when we book it.

Do I get a copy of the video?

You receive the video and our notes from the visit. We do not promise a specific file format, so tell us what your agent, buyer or the City needs when you book.

Does the City require an inspection in Sidney?

Only in project areas where the City has sent a notice. In those areas owners must have the building drain and building sewer inspected by video. Call Underground Utilities at 937-498-8157 if you are unsure.

Should I get a sewer inspection before buying a house?

For an older house, yes. The sewer lateral is underground and often not checked in a standard home inspection. A camera look can reveal cracks, roots or sags before you commit.

Can you inspect a line that is backing up?

Sometimes the line must be cleared first so the camera can see. Our 24/7 line is open if the line turns into a backup, and we can clear it and then run the camera.

Will the inspection fix the problem?

No. It shows what is wrong and where along the line. If the pipe needs work, we go over the repair options with you and give a clear price before any work begins.
