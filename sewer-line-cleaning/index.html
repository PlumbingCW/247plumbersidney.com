When sewage comes up in a basement drain, a tub or a toilet, stop running water everywhere in the house, keep people and pets out of the wet area, and call our 24/7 line at (855) 575-2890. Do not flush, run the washer or take a shower while you wait.

Sewer line cleaning is the clearing of the main drain that carries wastewater from your house to the City's sewer main. A cable or a high pressure water jet removes the blockage, and a camera can then show why it formed.

We answer backup calls at night, on weekends and on holidays, and a same-day visit is usually possible when the schedule allows. The sections below explain how we decide between cleaning and repair, and what is different for homes on septic.

Act first

Sewer backup: what to do first

Sewage is a health hazard. It can carry bacteria that cause stomach illness, so avoid touching it, keep children away, and wear rubber gloves and boots if you must go into the room. Wash hands well afterward and keep food and pet dishes out of the area.

Stop all water use, since every flush or load of laundry sends more wastewater toward the blockage. If standing water is near outlets, a furnace or a water heater, stay out and call us, because electricity and wet floors are dangerous together.

Open windows if the smell is strong. Do not pour chemical cleaner into a backed-up main, which does little against a blockage and adds a hazard to the water. Leave the floor drain alone and let the plumber open the cleanout.

Line cleaning

Sewer drain and main sewer cleaning

Sewer drain cleaning covers the building drain inside the house and the building sewer running out to the City's main. We usually reach it through a cleanout, a capped pipe on the floor or in the yard. If there is no cleanout, we may remove a toilet or work through a roof vent. A cleanout is worth adding if your home lacks one, since it makes every future cleaning faster.

Main sewer cleaning uses a heavier cable machine with cutting heads sized to the pipe. For grease, scale or roots that keep returning, we recommend hydro jetting, but only after a camera shows the pipe can take the pressure.

Know your system

City sewer or septic: which one you have

A home is either connected to a City sewer main or relies on a household septic tank and drain field. A septic problem is not a main line problem, and the work is different, so tell us on the first call if your home is on septic.

The Sidney-Shelby County Health Department permits and inspects household septic systems. Call it at 937-498-7249 with permit questions. We will clear a drain line that leads to a tank and tell you what we see. What we will not do is promise to diagnose or pump the tank over the phone, and we will say so up front if the job is one for a septic contractor registered with the Health District.

If you are unsure what you have, ask the Health Department or the City.

Clean water

Storm drains, roof water and the sanitary line

Sidney's City plan describes separate sanitary and storm systems. Rain from roofs and yards belongs in the storm system, not in the sewer that carries your toilet and sink water.

Downspouts and sump pumps tied into the sanitary line can overload it during heavy rain. The City's inflow and infiltration program treats those connections as inflow to be removed. If your sewer backs up mainly after storms, tell us.

Storm drain cleaning questions can be the City's responsibility or the owner's depending on where the drain sits. Call us and we help sort out which it is.

Local note

Where your responsibility ends in Sidney

The property owner is responsible for the sewer pipe from the building out to the City's main and tap. The City handles the main itself. That means a blockage in your own lateral is yours to pay to clear.

If you are unsure whether a problem is on your side, call the City's Underground Utilities office at 937-498-8157 while we start clearing. The City wastewater plant at 1091 Children's Home Rd treats what the system collects, but it does not clear your private line.

FAQs

Common questions

Do you handle sewer line cleaning emergencies at night or on weekends in Sidney?

Yes. Our line is open 24/7, including nights, weekends and holidays. If sewage is backing into the house we dispatch right away, and we tell you what to avoid until the plumber arrives.

Should I stay in the house during a sewage backup?

If the backup is small and contained, you can stay in unaffected rooms. If sewage is spreading, the smell is strong or water is near electrical items, leave the area and call. Keep children and pets out of the wet room.

How do I know if I have city sewer or septic?

Ask the City or the Sidney-Shelby County Health Department at 937-498-7249 if you are unsure. Tell us on the first call so we send the right equipment.

Who is responsible for the sewer line in Sidney?

The owner is responsible from the building to the City's main and tap. The City maintains the main. Call Underground Utilities at 937-498-8157 if you are unsure where your side ends.

Why does my sewer back up only when it rains?

Clean water from downspouts, sump pumps or foundation drains may be entering the sanitary line, or roots may be at a joint. Tell us the pattern and we look at both.

Will cleaning the line fix a broken pipe?

No. Cleaning clears a blockage. If a camera shows a crack, offset or collapse, the pipe needs repair, and we explain the options before any digging is planned.
